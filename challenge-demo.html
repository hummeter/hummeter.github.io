WHY YOU’D BOTHER

You cannot hold a steady note while you’re squeezing the club.

Tension shows up in your voice before you can feel it in your hands. So a flat line means a loose swing — and loose is what flushes it. Hum a steady note through the ball and the tension has nowhere to hide.

HOW TO DO IT

  1. Phone on the ground next to your ball, screen up.
  2. Tap start, then hum one single note — begin at address.
  3. Keep it going right through the ball. Don’t change the note, don’t get louder. Just hold it.

It stops on its own.

This is the screen you flagged. “Make a swing” is an instruction with no reason attached, and a stranger has no reason yet. The promise has to come before the ask — reduce tension, flush it — and then the three steps so nobody has to guess what “hum through your swing” means.

It is also your own line: the fix is never where you think. He came here to beat a score and leaves knowing his hands were the problem.

Hit a few in a rowBETA
Leave the phone down for a whole bucket.

What this does

Tap start once, put the phone down, and hit up to 8 balls. It listens the whole time and tries to find each swing on its own.

What it won’t do

It will miss some. It finds a swing by hearing the strike, and it doesn’t hear every one — quiet chips, wind, a mat. If it catches 5 of your 8, that’s the beta working. Turn it off and the meter goes back to one swing at a time.

Your call, and it’s the right one. As a default this would have been a disaster — we only detect the strike on 57% of confirmed real shots, so it would mis-cut roughly 4 swings in 10. Not one bad score you can shrug off: a whole bucket of them, with no way to tell which.

As an opt-in labelled BETA, that same 57% is fine — the person who flipped the switch was told, so a miss reads as the beta being a beta instead of the tool being broken. And it earns the number we need: whether it finds enough swings to ever be the default.

So don’t ask for six. Ask for two: your best and your worst. Those are the two a golfer genuinely remembers walking off — the flushed one and the chunked one. Two taps for a whole bucket.

And they happen to be the two the data is starving for. You have 9 rated shots and eight of them you rated an 8 — one bad strike in the entire set. A threshold like “your best strikes come above 78” needs a bottom half to draw the line against. Best-and-worst tagging produces exactly the spread that is missing — and it asks for less work than rating one shot does today.
